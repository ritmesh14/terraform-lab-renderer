## S003 — CONCEPT: Where this lab fits

This is lab twelve. Lab six tucked subnets inside the VNet block. The last two labs generated them from a map. Both work — but both assume subnets are interchangeable. When each tier needs different treatment, its own firewall, its own delegation, you write them out, one resource each. That's today's form.

## S004 — CONCEPT: Why the standalone form wins

- Inline subnets live inside the VNet block — compact, but closed
- Per-subnet resources (NSG, delegation) need a subnet to reference by id
- Standalone subnets are addressable: azurerm_subnet.web.id
- The pattern every later networking lab builds on

Why does the standalone form matter? Because a subnet that is its own resource has an identity — an I D that other resources can reference. An inline subnet belongs to the VNet; there's nothing to point a security group at. The moment a subnet needs to be treated as a thing — firewalled, delegated, peered — it has to stand alone. That's the form this lesson builds, and the form the coming N I C and N S G labs rely on.

## S011 — CONCEPT: Common pitfall — reaching back for inline subnets

- Inline subnets are fine for throwaway demos — but they close the door
- A per-subnet NSG or delegation needs a standalone subnet to reference
- Migrating inline → standalone later is surgery; start standalone
- for_each for many identical tiers; explicit blocks for distinct growing tiers

Here are the pitfalls in this lab. One: inline subnets are fine for throwaway demos — but they close the door. The inline block welds the subnet to the v net, so there's nothing standalone to build on. Two: a per-subnet n s g or delegation needs a standalone subnet to reference. The day you need a firewall on one tier, the inline block gives you nothing to attach it to. Three: migrating inline to standalone later is surgery — start standalone. Converting after the fact means untangling state that was never separate. Four: for each for many identical tiers; explicit blocks for distinct, growing tiers. Choose by shape — and when in doubt, standalone is the safer default.
